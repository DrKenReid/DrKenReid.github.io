The road so far

From Stirling to Ann Arbor

  1. Where I grew up

    A small town in Scotland, which gave me my directness, my dry sense of humour and a love of hills and the cold.

  2. 2009–2013

    BSc (Hons) in Computer Science, on a campus with a loch in the middle and a castle up the road.

  3. 2013–2015, Erskine

    Hewlett Packard Enterprise Services, in a small town just outside Glasgow. A mistaken email sent me to Trento to photograph a conference, and being unhappy at work turned a Ph.D. from "one day" into a plan.

  4. 2015–2019, Stirling

    Metaheuristics for real-world employee rostering: teaching computers to build workable shift patterns. Stirling will always be a home.

  5. Michigan State University, East Lansing

    A postdoc applying AI to genomics, satellite imagery, evolution and video games. I had never driven an automatic, or on the right.

  6. Ann Arbor

    Data scientist at the University of Michigan, right at the start of the generative AI boom.

  7. Now

    Ann Arbor, and home

    Senior Data Scientist at Rocket Mortgage, working on a master's in Organizational Leadership on the side. Michigan has been home long enough to feel like it.

What I try to live by

Mind what's mine, then look outward

I'm a Stoic in the everyday sense more than the marble-bust one. Epictetus is a favourite: a man born into slavery who worked out the one thing nobody could take from him, his own judgement and what he chose to do next. Most of what happens to me is out of my hands. My effort, my attention and how I treat people are not.

  1. 01

    What's in my control

    My effort, my judgements and what I do next. I try to spend my energy there and let the rest go, which is much easier to write than to do.

  2. 02

    Spending it on other people

    Not fighting what I can't change leaves room for the people in front of me. I would rather assume someone is having a hard day than that they are a bad person, and ask what would help.

  3. 03

    In the work

    It's why this site is built accessibility first, why I write about how we use AI rather than whether it is good or bad, and why I ask who a system leaves out before who it serves.
